## 4. Making a Sale

This is what you'll do most often â€” someone walks in to buy something.

**Steps:**

1. Click **New Sale**. A form opens.
2. **Customer**: if it's a regular customer, type their name and pick them from the list. If you don't pick anyone, it automatically uses "Walk-in Customer" â€” fine for someone you don't know.
3. **Payment Method**: tap one â€”
   - **Cash** â€” they're paying you cash right now
   - **UPI** â€” they paid you by phone/UPI right now
   - **Bank Transfer** â€” they paid by bank transfer / NEFT / RTGS right now
   - **Credit** â€” they're taking the goods now and will pay you later (see [Section 6](#6-selling-on-credit--how-it-works))
   - **Other** â€” any other payment not listed above
4. Click **Add a line** to add what they're buying.
5. Type the product name and pick it from the list.
6. The system automatically picks the oldest stock first (so nothing expires sitting in your store).
7. Type how much they're buying (the quantity).
8. The price fills in automatically â€” change it if you're giving a special price.
9. If you're giving a discount, type the percentage in the **Discount %** box.
10. Add more lines if they're buying more than one item.
11. Check the **Total Amount** at the bottom right â€” that's what to collect.
12. Click **Confirm & Print Receipt**.

That's it. Your stock goes down automatically, and a receipt opens that you can print and hand to the customer.

**Why it works this way:** You don't have to think about which batch to sell or do any maths â€” the system does the stock and the totals for you. You just need to know what's being bought and how it's being paid for.

## 5. Receiving Stock From a Supplier

When new stock arrives at your shop:

**Why the Cost Price matters:** The system uses the cost price you enter here to calculate your profit on every sale. If you don't enter a cost price, the system will still work â€” but it won't be able to show you accurate profit figures on the Dashboard. Always enter the cost price.

## 6. Selling on Credit â€” How It Works

Some customers (especially regular farmers) will want to "put it on their account" and pay you later. This is what **Credit** payment method is for.

**What happens when you sell on Credit:**

- The sale goes through immediately, stock goes down, receipt prints â€” exactly the same as a cash sale.
- But the system marks that bill as **unpaid**, and remembers it.
- Every customer has a **Credit Period** â€” normally 30 days (you can change this per customer, see [Section 10](#10-adding-a-new-customer)). The bill becomes **overdue** if it isn't paid within that many days.
- The printed receipt for a Credit sale shows in red: **"Amount Due: â‚¹X by [date]"** â€” so the customer themselves has a reminder of when they need to pay you.

**Cash, UPI, Bank Transfer, and Other are different:** these are treated as paid in full the moment you confirm the sale â€” there's nothing to chase later, because the money's already in hand.

## 7. Receiving Payment From a Customer

When a credit customer comes to pay you back (in full or in part):

1. Find the customer â€” either through **Top Customers** or by searching Contacts.
2. Open their record.
3. Click the **Receive Payment** button (it's near their name, with the village and crops information).
4. Type how much they're paying you.
5. Pick how they're paying (Cash / UPI / Other).
6. Save.

**What happens automatically:** if this customer has more than one unpaid bill, the payment is applied to their **oldest** unpaid bill first. If there's money left over after that bill is fully paid, it automatically rolls onto their next-oldest bill, and so on.

**Made a mistake?** If you entered the wrong amount, open that payment from the customer's **Payments** button and delete it â€” the bills it was applied to go back to being owed, exactly as before. Then enter the correct payment again.

## 14. Profit and Analytics â€” Understanding the Numbers

The system automatically calculates your **profit on every sale**. It does this using the cost price you entered when receiving stock and the selling price you charged the customer.

**How it's calculated:**

```
Profit per item = (Selling Price - Cost Price) Ã— Quantity Sold
Total Profit    = Sum of all line item profits in the bill
```

If a customer got a discount, that reduces the selling price before the calculation â€” so discounts correctly reduce your profit.

**Where to see it:**

- **Dashboard â†’ Profit tab** â€” daily, monthly, yearly, lifetime profit totals
- **Dashboard â†’ Profit tab â†’ Cost Price Analysis** â€” sales vs cost vs margin breakdown
- **Dashboard â†’ Charts tab â†’ Sales by Category** â€” which categories drive profit

**What "Profit Margin %" means:**

If your sales this month are â‚¹10,00,000 and your cost was â‚¹8,30,000, then:
- Gross Profit = â‚¹1,70,000
- Profit Margin = 17%

This percentage tells you how efficiently your shop runs. A margin below 10% is worth looking at â€” are prices too low, or costs too high?

## 17. Stock Reports â€” Dead Stock, Valuation, Audit

### Dead Stock

Click **Products â†’ Dead Stock**.

## 19. Common Questions

**Q: I sold something on Credit by mistake â€” it should have been Cash.**
A: Open the sale from **History â†’ Sales**, but note that the amount/date/items on a confirmed sale can't be edited (this protects your receipt records from accidental changes). The cleanest fix is to record a payment for the full amount right away through **Receive Payment** â€” that settles it immediately, the same as if it had been a cash sale from the start.

**Q: A customer paid me extra by mistake / I want to record an advance.**
A: You can still enter the payment â€” if they have no unpaid bills (or you pay more than they owe), the extra amount is simply recorded as paid, ready to apply against their next bill.

**Q: Why did a bill suddenly turn red overnight?**
A: The system checks every bill once a day automatically, and updates a bill to "overdue" the moment it crosses its due date â€” even if nobody touched the computer that day.

**Q: The profit on the Dashboard is wrong â€” it's showing â‚¹0.**
A: This usually means no cost price was entered when receiving stock. Go to the batch (in **Batches â†’ All Batches**), find the relevant batch, and check if the **Cost Price** field is filled in. Going forward, always enter the cost price when you receive stock â€” the system can only calculate profit if it knows what you paid.

**Q: Why is a product showing "Never Sold" in Dead Stock?**
A: The product was received into stock but never sold through the shop. Either it's a new product you haven't started selling yet, or it got received by mistake. Check the product and decide whether to keep it, return it to the supplier, or remove it from the system.

**Q: Can I trust the totals?**
A: Yes â€” every total (stock, money owed, money paid, profit) is calculated by the system itself from your actual sales and payments. You never need to do this maths by hand.

**Q: The charts are not showing on the Dashboard.**
A: Your internet connection may be slow â€” the charts need to load from the internet. Wait a few seconds and refresh the page. If the charts still don't appear, ask your computer person to check the internet connection.

**Q: What if I'm not sure about something?**
A: Nothing in this system deletes your sales history. If you're ever unsure, it's safe to look around â€” the worst that can happen is you click into a screen you didn't mean to. Just click **Agro Inventory** at the top-left to go back to the main menu.
